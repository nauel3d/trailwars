# TRAIL WARS — INDEPENDENT UI/UX RESEARCH & CREATIVE DIRECTION

## Your assignment

You are taking over as the **Creative Director, Senior Roblox UI/UX Designer, Visual Design Researcher, and Player Acquisition Strategist** for TRAIL WARS.

Our current UI is functional, but I am dissatisfied with its visual quality. It looks too plain, generic, and unexciting.

I want you to independently determine what a truly outstanding Roblox game interface should look like in 2026.

**Do not ask me to pick colors, fonts, themes, layouts, or design inspirations before you've done the research. That is your job.**

Do not simply polish the current interface.

I want you to challenge the entire visual direction and discover something substantially better.

## Project context

Repository: https://github.com/nauel3d/trailwars

The project already contains a working Roblox UI foundation built using native Luau components and Rojo.

It also contains research on Curve Fever Pro, existing menus, customization, monetization foundations, navigation, settings, and UI tests.

Preserve all existing work.

The game is a fast-paced, top-down multiplayer arcade survival game where players steer moving cores, leave trails, trap opponents, and try to become the last survivor.

It does not require Roblox avatars, a physical 3D lobby, or humanoid characters.

The intended experience is simple to understand, exciting to play, and optimized for mobile, desktop, and controller.

We want an original game identity, not a Curve Fever clone.

# PHASE 1 — RESEARCH WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS ON ROBLOX

Before designing anything, conduct serious research into current Roblox game presentation, UI quality, visual appeal, and player acquisition.

Study successful and emerging Roblox experiences relevant to:

- Casual competitive multiplayer games.
- Fast arcade games.
- Short-session PvP games.
- Skill-based games.
- Popular games with strong visual identities.
- Games with effective cosmetic shops.
- Games that convert visitors into active players.
- Games with excellent mobile interfaces.

Do not study only games in our exact genre. Investigate why visually successful Roblox games in other genres attract attention.

Use current information and distinguish measurable evidence from subjective design judgments.

## Research questions

Investigate:

1. What makes someone click a Roblox game icon or thumbnail?
2. What visual characteristics make a Roblox game immediately recognizable?
3. How do successful Roblox games communicate their core gameplay in one glance?
4. What UI styles are currently popular, and which are becoming generic or overused?
5. How do successful games make their Play buttons prominent?
6. How do they design menus without overwhelming younger players?
7. How do they communicate progression, rewards, currency, and customization?
8. How do their shops present cosmetics in an attractive but understandable way?
9. What makes a game feel premium rather than like a generic Roblox simulator?
10. How do excellent interfaces adapt to small phones?
11. What makes victory screens and rewards satisfying?
12. How do visuals, loading times, accessibility, and perceived quality influence retention?
13. Which design approaches are technically realistic in Roblox Studio?
14. What design decisions would make TRAIL WARS stand out?

Research Roblox's official Creator Hub documentation, relevant platform analytics guidance, actual Roblox game examples, and credible professional game UI design resources.

Do not claim a design increases clicks, conversion, or revenue without supporting data. Form hypotheses where direct evidence is unavailable.

## Competitor analysis

Choose a meaningful sample of relevant Roblox games.

For every game, record:

- Game name and URL.
- Why it was selected.
- Actual game icon and thumbnail presentation.
- Homepage or menu structure, where verifiable.
- Typography, color, composition, contrast, and visual hierarchy.
- Progression and store presentation.
- Mobile considerations.
- Distinctive design strengths.
- Weaknesses or overused patterns.
- What lessons can be applied to TRAIL WARS without copying.

Make a comparative research report.

Do not invent screenshots or interface details for games you cannot inspect.

# PHASE 3 — DEVELOP ORIGINAL CREATIVE DIRECTIONS

Using your research, independently create at least **three substantially different, high-quality visual directions** for TRAIL WARS.

These must not simply be the same UI recolored three times.

Each direction must have its own visual philosophy, personality, composition, branding approach, and presentation strategy.

You decide what they should be.

Do not default automatically to neon cyberpunk because the game features trails.

Explore unexpected visual approaches if your research supports them.

For each direction, define:

- Core creative concept.
- Intended audience appeal.
- Visual personality.
- Logo treatment.
- Color system.
- Typography.
- Shape language.
- Navigation structure.
- Button treatment.
- Background composition.
- Cosmetic presentation.
- Motion principles.
- Mobile adaptation.
- Technical feasibility.
- Performance implications.
- Expected advantages and risks.

Critically compare the directions.

Recommend the strongest candidate, but do not finalize it without my approval.

**One-of-one means a distinctive and cohesive identity, not excessive effects or visual clutter.**

# PHASE 4 — GENERATE ACTUAL VISUAL MOCKUPS

This is a mandatory phase.

**Do not implement your design directly in Roblox Studio before I see and approve it.**

Use the best available image-generation or design tools to create high-fidelity visual mockups for each creative direction.

Use actual generated images, not descriptions, ASCII diagrams, or generic wireframes.

At minimum, generate:

1. Main menu / Home.
2. Shop.
3. Customization / Inventory.
4. Profile / Progression.
5. Settings.
6. Match HUD.
7. Elimination / Spectator.
8. Victory / Results.
9. A representative mobile version.

Where appropriate, create individual full-resolution screen mockups rather than putting every screen into one crowded collage.

Create a consistent visual language across each direction.

The designs must look like realistic, polished game interfaces that could be implemented in Roblox—not arbitrary concept art with fake controls.

Ensure that the game depicted is actually TRAIL WARS: moving cores and top-down trail survival, not an unrelated avatar racing or combat game.

Do not invent gameplay systems simply to make a mockup more exciting.

Any speculative features must be explicitly marked as proposals.

Store the generated images and design specifications in the project workspace.

# PHASE 7 — IMPLEMENTATION AFTER APPROVAL ONLY

Once I explicitly approve the design, translate the approved visuals into a working Roblox UI.

Treat the approved mockups as the visual specification.

Do not produce a simplified, generic approximation.

Use Roblox-native UI components, suitable assets, and the existing Luau/Rojo architecture.

Preserve the existing navigation, inventory, economy, and monetization foundations unless a redesign requires changes.

Implement the appearance consistently across all screens.

Ensure the following:

- Responsive mobile layouts.
- Keyboard, mouse, and controller navigation.
- Proper safe-area handling.
- Accessible text.
- Efficient rendering.
- Minimal unnecessary animation costs.
- Reusable UI components.
- Correct button interactions.
- Proper screen transitions.
- Accurate cosmetic previews.
- Clear loading and purchase states.
- Server-authoritative data and purchases.

Compare the implemented UI against the approved mockups using screenshots.

Iterate until the visual result closely matches what was approved.

Where Roblox technical limitations require an adjustment, explain the tradeoff rather than silently changing the design.

Run the existing tests and add appropriate visual checks.

Commit verified changes to GitHub.

# CREDIT AND TOOL EFFICIENCY

Previous work used too many computer-use and runtime inspection calls.

Use a cost-efficient workflow.

Prioritize:

1. Existing repository research and evidence.
2. Official documentation and conventional web research.
3. Existing screenshots and game references.
4. Image generation for design exploration.
5. Source-code tools and local automation.
6. Bundled Roblox Studio verification only when implementation begins.

Avoid unnecessarily repeating the Steam/REA investigation.

Do not continuously launch Roblox Studio or the reference game just to inspect ordinary design details.

Use expensive GUI tools only when their information is genuinely necessary.

Do not sacrifice final design quality merely to minimize tool calls.

- Research first, then design.

- Do not choose a final direction without my approval.
- Do not implement UI before approval.

- Do not use fake player statistics or monetization figures as research evidence.
- Do not ignore mobile players.

- Do not claim popularity is guaranteed.
- Do not delete or overwrite existing working systems.
- Do not spend excessive credits on repetitive computer-use operations.
- Save your research, image mockups, design files, and decisions so the process remains reproducible.
