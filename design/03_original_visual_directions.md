# Original visual directions — provisional exploration

The reference audit remains open. These are independently authored alternatives, not a selected brand, approved palette, mascot, or sellable bundle. The current implementation uses a neutral wireframe treatment so system work can proceed without passing the audit gate prematurely. Nothing below reuses reference artwork, names of cosmetics, audio, typography treatments, layout, or source.

The common design problem is seeing a small moving core and its collision boundary instantly while understanding only two steering actions. Home navigation must prioritize Play; customization should demonstrate the same silhouette and trail footprint that opponents see.

| Direction | Visual language | Readability | Memorability | Implementation / mobile cost | Cosmetic preview quality | Principal risk and test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Course markings | Broad flat surfaces, numerical lane markers, purposeful linework and asymmetrical route diagrams. Interface resembles a compact race card rather than a space dashboard. Color family remains undecided. | Excellent potential: large labels, little decoration, clear borders. | Distinctive if one route-marking motif is consistently used. No mascot required. | Low: Frames, text and a few static vector-like line segments; no blur, glow or background video. | Honest overhead miniature course with a single moving core; static snapshot fallback. | Must avoid implying physical lanes constrain steering. In comprehension test, ask players to describe movement before tutorial. |
| B. Cut-paper arena | Tactile flat shapes, offset edges and playful stamped result cards; core is a simple layered mark. Palette and material texture remain undecided. | Strong separation of foreground and background, but offset shadows must not confuse trail hitboxes. | High potential without relying on neon or spaceship imagery. | Moderate: several decorative layers; restrict to menus and freeze in reduced motion. No actual paper textures needed in MVP. | Cosmetic cards show silhouette, trail pattern and elimination stamp at identical scale. | Decorations may make collision boundaries ambiguous. Compare collision judgment with decorations removed. |
| C. Signal instruments | Restrained schematic diagrams, measured spacing and a small kinetic signal motif. Original utility language rather than military or science-fiction imitation. | Strong in HUD if text remains human and labels avoid technical jargon. | Moderate to high; a consistent core signal could become recognizable. | Low to moderate: thin linework must survive small screens; static patterns preferable to scanning animations. | Side-by-side neutral and selected core with line-pattern legend; no 3D object viewer necessary. | Fine strokes may disappear on inexpensive phones. Test at 360 logical pixels and reduce layers before adding effects. |

All three use the same independent information hierarchy: one primary action; one sentence explaining play; persistent Back; cosmetics previewed on a small core; no commercial banners on home. They differ in shape grammar, surface treatment, composition and motion, so they can be compared meaningfully after research closes.

Selection protocol: finish or explicitly block every discovered reference branch; test three low-fidelity examples with new players on desktop and narrow mobile; measure first-click success, explanation of steering, recognition after a delay and collision-boundary accuracy. Score readability 35%, comprehension 25%, performance feasibility 20%, recognition 10%, preview usefulness 10%. These weights are a proposal. Reject any direction that sacrifices boundary visibility even if preference scores are higher.

Final decision: **DEFERRED**. No final brand colors, mascot, theme or monetization bundle is selected in this deliverable.
