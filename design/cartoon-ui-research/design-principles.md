# Trail Wars design principles

1. **Trails explain the game.** They are continuous readable paths, not wallpaper or confetti. A turn in the composition should point toward a racer or an action.
2. **A core is a character at two scales.** At inspection size: silhouette, visor, eyes, fin and one glint. At match size: reliable colored center, readable heading and ID. Never enlarge art in a mockup to disguise a small actual arena.
3. **Depth means touch.** Reserve strongest shadows and compression for PLAY, powers and rematch. Supporting surfaces are quiet; navigation is mostly words/icons. Do not put every sentence into the same outlined rounded rectangle.
4. **One primary action per state.** Ready → Play; alive → steer/powers; eliminated → pilot/spectate; final → rematch. Focus is distinct from selected/equipped/owned.
5. **Handcrafted is intentional, not random.** Three controlled surface classes; original vehicle/rocket silhouettes; consistent outline weights. No arbitrary blob behind every group, decorative currency, fake badges or grid of unrelated clipart.
6. **Quiet play, expressive consequence.** Short local press, confirmed hit and OUT response. Bigger character poses and score races belong between rounds and after the match. Inputs never wait.
7. **Truthful state beats celebration.** A power hit is not a kill. Pending is not success. Preview is not equipped. Sample data is labeled. No current live persistence guarantee, leagues or purchases are invented.
8. **Compose per usable rectangle.** Home never page-scrolls. Compact landscape is not portrait shrunk. Keep the 1300:800 world, safe controls and minimum text. Decorative illustrations yield first.
9. **Accessible without effect.** Rank numerals, names, timers and cooldown numbers remain. Reduced motion stops travel, bounce and ambient demo; quality is a separate preference. Sound is additive.
10. **Approval is a phase boundary.** Editable design assets may change here. Game/UI source, server, economy, sound uploads and live places do not. Explicit visual approval is required before implementation.
