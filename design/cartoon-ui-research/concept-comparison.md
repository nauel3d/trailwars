# Three directions, three construction rules

These are authored alternatives, not implementations. All colors and illustrations are original. Shared honest match data and board ratio allow comparison without inventing a more flattering game.

## A — Candy Comet Arcade — recommended, subject to approval

**Identity:** warm cream, sea-glass mint, navy, coral, cornflower and marigold. Finned, rounded comets; short hard shadows; a subtle glint on touched surfaces. Fredoka One headings + Arial body/numbers. Curving trails are the compositional spine.

**Composition:** open asymmetrical Home; prominent character/PLAY; navigation becomes a quiet shelf. Match arena left, quiet standings and powers right. Winner gets an illustrated stage and separate score list. On portrait, identity/short standings occupy the space between board and fixed thumb controls.

**Why it works:** preserves the recognizable SNAP palette and matches an already functioning native art/motion foundation. PHIGHTING's character-first staging and Dandy's quiet active-play hierarchy support this direction. Rounded controls are retained where they communicate touch, not used as a universal container.

**Weakness:** easiest to make generic if every support element inherits the CTA capsule. Small live racers cannot show large illustration detail; the mockups deliberately retain that limitation. Needs a distinctive silhouette and disciplined three-surface rule. The original source-art face remains a useful fallback; a 3D migration is not required.

## B — Stickerbook Showdown

**Identity:** oatmeal paper, olive-black ink, dusty tomato, denim and ochre. Kalam lettering, angular die-cut controls, slightly irregular frames, sticker-edged racers and masking-tape labels. Flat print rather than gloss.

**Composition:** Home is a pasted racing poster; desktop HUD uses a side race-notes sheet and broad playboard; final results form a round-by-round scrapbook, not A's hero/list split. Mobile groups standings in a single taped sheet.

**Why consider it:** most visibly handmade; original cutout racers integrate naturally with trail marks. Block Tales' coherent substrate and Super Doomspire's collectible-object vocabulary support the hypothesis. Snipperclips supplies a shape-as-character precedent, not copied artwork.

**Weakness:** Kalam must stay out of tiny competitive numbers. Irregular edges need a modest image set; ubiquitous paper texture adds noise. The oversized sticker rim must disappear in active play. It is a larger departure from approved Candy Comet, and less natural for a later dimensional vehicle.

## C — Miniature Toy Arena

**Identity:** powder-blue tabletop, enamel cream, coral and butter-yellow, navy joins. Molded capsule vehicles, recessed arena, plinths and physical score tokens. Fredoka One labels + Arial detail.

**Composition:** board centered between physical ability paddles; a token row replaces the right standings column. Results use a podium and a separate rematch console. Mobile compresses tokens and stacks controls; this is the weakest of the three for compact-phone efficiency.

**Why consider it:** strongest future fit for original 3D hover-racers and satisfying placement ceremonies. One selected object in Tower Heroes and physical-placement presentation in party games motivate the direction.

**Weakness:** biggest performance/asset risk; nested lips and highlights easily become excessive framing. A literal tabletop camera must not distort the competitive board. Recommend a 2D orthographic board with a decorative rim, not a new 3D lobby, camera or gameplay renderer. C's extra depth earns its place mainly on Results.

## Rubric

Scores are art-direction judgments, 1–5, not user testing or measured performance. Weight emphasizes play readability and mobile fit. Five means particularly strong; two means a real concern. Identical sample state is used in all concepts.

| Native Roblox feasibility | 6 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| Expected performance economy | 4 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Fit for later original 3D vehicles | 2 | 4 | 3 | 5 |

A is the pragmatic recommendation: the largest visual improvement can come from better composition, consistent silhouettes, art assets at useful sizes and selective depth, while preserving source foundations. B is the stronger alternate if handmade paper expression matters more than continuity. C should be rejected as the immediate full-game direction unless its extra asset and mobile cost are acceptable. The recommendation authorizes no implementation.

## Approval scope

Review the Home, realistic HUD, inter-round and final Results first. Approve or revise the **direction and components**, not an illustration flattened into the game. Nine screen families are developed for A; B/C establish alternatives rather than promising equal production completeness. Approval must explicitly precede any Roblox UI work.
